# Team battle: beginner guide

Generated from the same rule text used on the game page.

## Objective and victory

Each team starts with 250 tickets. The match ends when a side runs out of tickets or after 120 turns. If time expires, it is a draw regardless of tickets; if tickets run out earlier, the side with more remaining wins.

Each eliminated soldier costs 1 ticket. Destroyed tanks cost an additional 15; IFVs and APCs 10; helicopters and logistics vehicles 5. Crew and passenger casualties are charged separately. For example, losing a tank with three crew costs up to 18 tickets.

## Time and match controls

One turn is one simulated minute and lasts 5 real seconds. A full 120-turn match takes about 10 minutes without pauses. Tickets can end a match earlier.

Start / Pause controls time. You can issue orders while paused; execution starts next turn. Capturing an objective does not pause the match.

## How to issue an order

1. Tap a squad on the map or in an objective side list. 2. Select one of the four order buttons. 3. Choose A, B or C. Orders apply immediately without confirmation. On phones and computers, the selected squad, its orders and objectives are in the panel above the map.

Changing objective preserves the order; a flank route restarts via its waypoint. Squad orders also apply to its vehicle. Individual vehicle destination controls in the details panel take priority; a new squad order clears that override. Keep logistics close to infantry to resupply it.

### Attack

Advance to the chosen objective, stopping 3 units from its center. Firepower +15%, normal movement speed. Use for assaults and capture.

### Hold

Move more slowly to an assigned position 6 units from the objective center. Firepower −15%. On arrival, incoming infantry fire is reduced by 32%, vehicle fire against infantry by 35%, and capture pressure rises by 14%. Cover bonuses do not apply during approach.

### Flank

First reach a flank waypoint offset by 12 horizontally and 14 vertically, then approach the far side of the objective. Movement is faster; after the waypoint firepower rises by 12%. Capture pressure is reduced by 8%. This is a two-stage route.

### Recon

Observe from the near edge, offset by 12 horizontally and 8 vertically. Base detection range is 34 rather than 27, with firepower −25%. Scouts in the squad and terrain further affect detection.

## Objectives A, B and C

A is forest, B village, C ridge. Ready personnel within less than 22 map units capture objectives, including active crews and passengers of functioning vehicles. Empty or destroyed vehicles cannot capture. Shorter distance increases capture pressure.

The side with more ready personnel in the zone, including active crews and passengers of functioning vehicles, captures; equal numbers stop progress. A smaller numerical advantage means slower capture. Colour shows ownership; the bar shows progress and its direction. A BLUE / RED progress bar alone does not mean ownership changed: capture must complete.

Each turn the side holding fewer objectives loses tickets equal to the difference. At 2:1 it loses 1 ticket per turn; at 3:0 it loses 3. Equal objective counts cause no bleed. Capturing does not restore spent tickets.

## Squads and reserves

Infantry squads have up to 9 members. Each tank squad is a separate crew of up to 3; IFV and APC squads have up to 2. The commander counts toward the limit. Logistics and helicopter personnel are grouped separately.

Every squad needs an eligible officer; advisors do not count. A match cannot start without enough commanders. Equal roster sizes balances active soldier counts. Reserves do not take part in this match. Equal headcounts do not guarantee equal vehicle strength.

## Ammunition and logistics

Infantry starts with 200 rounds per member: 9 soldiers means 1800 rounds in a shared pool. Fire against one enemy squad uses up to 15 rounds per ready soldier per turn. An anti-tank volley uses up to 30 from the same pool. Insufficient ammo weakens fire; at zero the squad cannot shoot.

Main base refills the pool within 10 units. A functioning logistics vehicle with at least one active crew member refills infantry within 12. While nearby it resupplies automatically every turn. Trucks have no separate finite cargo or loading requirement.

Every 5 turns, the shared pool gains 5 rounds per active squad member, up to capacity. For example, 9 ready soldiers add 45 rounds. Returning soldiers add 200 rounds to the shared pool. Vehicles currently have unlimited ammunition. There are no HABs, construction or repairs.

## How combat works

Squads fire automatically at detected targets within range: infantry at less than 25 units, anti-tank fire at less than 18. Ready soldiers, distance, orders, terrain, morale, suppression, ammo and leader reputation affect results. Randomness means similar situations need not produce identical casualties.

Both sides’ infantry volleys are calculated before applying their casualties, new suppression and morale loss. Armor and anti-tank fire run in an earlier phase. Tanks have an advantage over IFVs and APCs, while living infantry anti-tank specialists can also damage vehicles.

Forest, village and ridge affect detection, protection and speed. Terrain zones are marked around objectives; illustrated rivers, roads and buildings do not each have separate passability rules. Suppression reduces fire and speed; morale affects fire. Suppression fades and morale recovers over turns.

## Commanders and reputation

The assigned squad leader’s reputation, on a 0–25 scale, sets squad effectiveness. Other members’ reputations do not affect it. Unrated leaders use a neutral 12.5 / 25.

While the leader is out, squad effectiveness falls by 20%. A commander death reduces team firepower and capture for the next 3 turns (≈15 seconds without pauses): Second Lieutenant −2%, Lieutenant −3%, First Lieutenant −4%, Captain −6%, Major −8%, Lieutenant Colonel −10%, Colonel −15%, General −20%. Only the strongest concurrent penalty applies; each expires independently. Losing any commander does not itself end the match: play continues while tickets and time remain. A red alert announces the loss; a status shows the remaining penalty.

## Casualties, transport and returns

There are no wounded or healing. Eliminated soldiers are temporarily unavailable. Their delay is 3 turns plus distance from squad to own base divided by 10, rounded up. For example, 40 units means 7 turns. Reinforcements currently rejoin their squad directly without a separate map journey.

Destroying a vehicle eliminates its active crew and passengers. A replacement appears stopped at own base after 20 turns (about 100 seconds without pauses), at full health. Issue another order to move it. Vehicles below 50% health move more slowly.

Infantry can board a free functioning vehicle with sufficient capacity within 15 units, with no enemy within 18 of squad or vehicle. IFVs, APCs and helicopters carry 8 passengers, logistics trucks 12; tanks carry none. Embarked infantry cannot fire, but active passengers participate in capturing objectives. If an enemy comes within 18 units of the vehicle, passengers disembark automatically.

## Map, modes and saving

The gold line is the selected squad’s current route segment: first a waypoint, then the far side for Flank. Dashed arrows show other squads on the selected side. At less than 9 units from an objective, icons move into vertical lists: blue on the left and red on the right. Lists grow to a capped height, then scroll independently. Small coloured dots show true locations; moving icons does not change combat calculations.

Enemies are shown only when detected. A question mark is a last known position, retained for up to 5 turns and possibly outdated. On phones, the whole map fits the screen width. Use the squad list when markers overlap.

Against the computer you control Blue. The computer distributes squads between objectives and reacts to detected enemies. In Two players, one screen, select a visible squad of the desired side. You can switch sides with the button in the panel above the map, or with X on a computer. Both players use the same device. Roster settings apply to new matches. Progress saves in this browser. A running match does not pause when changing tabs or sections; after reload, elapsed time is calculated automatically. Results do not change reputation or ranks on the main site.

## Your first match: step by step

Choose two teams. Equal rosters balances active headcounts, not vehicle strength. Against the computer you control BLUE; local mode lets both players use one device. Create a match, select a squad, choose Attack and an objective, then Start. Begin with two objectives rather than chasing all three.

Watch tickets, ready personnel and ammunition. After capturing an objective, use Hold. Move logistics close to infantry. Use Pause when you need time to issue orders. Capture does not stop the match, and closing a running match is not a substitute for manual pause.

## Reading the screen

The scoreboard shows remaining tickets, ready personnel and simulated time. A/B/C cards show ownership, progress and detected nearby personnel. The panel above the map controls the selected squad, order and objective. On compact cards, № is the squad number, pers. is ready personnel, and the insignia identifies the commander rank; a dim insignia means the commander is out.

The squad list helps select crowded units. Squad details show the commander, personnel, morale, suppression, ammunition and transport. The log explains casualties, penalties and captures; recent engagement calculations help explain damage. The chart shows ticket spending, and reserves are people outside this battle. Long objective lists scroll independently without enlarging the map.

## Practical advice

Holding two objectives against one drains 1 enemy ticket per turn: 20 tickets over 20 turns before combat losses. Avoid trading a tank and crew for an uncertain assault: losing a tank with three crew costs up to 18 tickets. Recon improves detection but reduces firepower. Hold provides cover only after reaching its position.

Equal personnel in the zone stop capture. Count ready people, including crews, rather than icons. Protect logistics: nine ready soldiers regenerate only 45 rounds every five turns, while firing at one enemy squad can consume up to 135 per turn. The time limit produces a draw even when your team leads on tickets.

## Simulation limits and weaknesses

This is an abstract simulation rather than Squad physics. Background roads, the river and individual buildings do not create obstacles or cover. Cover and detection depend on abstract terrain zones. Personnel counts determine capture direction, so a small crew cannot match a full infantry squad. Flight altitude is not modeled separately: active helicopter crews and passengers in the zone also contribute to capture.

Logistics and headquarters refill infantry to capacity for free each turn; cargo never runs out and no loading is required. Vehicles have unlimited ammunition. This makes protected logistics especially powerful. Passive regeneration is an abstract mechanic without a physical ammunition source. There are no repairs, construction, HABs or rally points.

Combat phases are ordered: armor and anti-tank fire precede infantry volleys; infantry volleys are calculated before applying their casualties. Random rounding is particularly noticeable in small squads. Equal headcounts do not guarantee balanced assets, and member reputation does not strengthen a squad: leader reputation matters. One win does not establish a superior tactic; compare several scenario codes.

The computer weighs objectives, distances, assigned forces and detected enemies. It does not plan a long campaign or optimize transport and logistics like a human. Match state and calculations live in the browser and can be edited through developer tools. There is no server validation or network multiplayer, so results are unsuitable for a tamper-resistant ranking.
